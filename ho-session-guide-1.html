Objective
Teach the client to update their monitoring dashboard independently. First you demonstrate. Then they drive. They must update the dashboard themselves at least once during this session — watching you is not enough.
Key Rule
You have 20 minutes for this tool. Spend the first 8 minutes demonstrating. Spend the remaining 12 minutes watching them do it. Do not spend more than 8 minutes on your screen — hand control over early.
Do
Share your screen. Open Tab 1 — their monitoring dashboard. Give a 2-minute overview of the full layout before touching anything.
"This is your monitoring dashboard. You use this once a month. Only the yellow cells are editable — every other cell calculates automatically. The dashboard tracks your Sharpe ratio, geographic concentration, property type allocation, debt maturity schedule, VaR, and how far you have drifted from your optimal allocation. Red flags appear automatically when any metric moves outside its target range. Let me show you a full update."
Do
Walk through updating one full month of data yourself — out loud, explaining every cell as you update it. Take 5–6 minutes maximum on your demonstration.
"I am now updating the monthly inputs for all 6 properties. Property 1 — current rent this month [amount], current occupancy [status], mortgage payment [amount]. Watch how the Sharpe ratio recalculates automatically as I enter each figure. Property 2..."
Watch
Stop sharing your screen. Ask them to share their screen and open their copy of the dashboard. Give them a specific month to update — use slightly different numbers so they cannot just copy what they watched.
"Your turn. Share your screen and open your copy of the dashboard. I want you to update it as if it is July 2026. I will give you the numbers to use as we go — you just enter them. Start with Property 1. Rent this month: $2,850. Occupancy: fully occupied. Mortgage payment: same as always. Go ahead."
Watch
Watch without interrupting unless they are about to make an error. Let them work through it. Silence is fine — they are learning by doing, not by listening to you.
Do
After they finish the update — point out one specific flag or insight the updated dashboard reveals. Connect it to something real in their portfolio.
"Look at your geographic concentration metric now that you have updated it. It has moved from 58% to 61% in Dallas. That is approaching the 65% warning threshold. This is exactly the kind of drift the dashboard is designed to catch monthly before it becomes a problem."
Ask
Confirm independence before moving on.
"On the first Monday of every month when you sit down with this dashboard — do you feel confident you can update it completely on your own? What questions do you still have about any part of it?"
Dashboard notes — where they struggled, what questions they asked

Monte Carlo Simulator

0–5 Open
5–25 Dashboard

Objective
Teach the client to run their Monte Carlo simulator on a hypothetical new property. By the end they must have run the model themselves — with you watching — and be able to interpret the output correctly without your help.
Do
Share your screen. Open Tab 2 — their Monte Carlo model. Give a 2-minute overview of the inputs and what the model does before running anything.
"This is your Monte Carlo simulator. It is pre-loaded with your 6 existing properties and all their data. When you are evaluating a potential new acquisition you drop in the property details here — value, rent, expenses, debt, interest rate — and tap Run. The model runs 10,000 simulations and shows you the full IRR distribution of your portfolio with that property added. It tells you the median outcome, the best case, the worst case, and how the new property affects your Sharpe ratio and efficient frontier position."
Do
Run one scenario yourself — out loud, narrating every input as you enter it. Use the scenario you prepared in Phase 1.
"I am going to model a hypothetical Nashville single family home. Value: $450,000. Monthly rent: $2,800. Monthly operating expenses: $900. Mortgage balance: $280,000. Monthly payment: $1,680. Interest rate: 6.8% fixed. I tap Run. Watch the output."
Do
Walk through interpreting the output together — median IRR, Sharpe ratio change, efficient frontier movement. Show them what a good result looks like versus a bad result.
"The output shows that adding this Nashville property moves your median portfolio IRR from 6.8% to 7.1%. Your Sharpe ratio improves from 0.54 to 0.61. Your correlation with existing holdings is 0.42 — well below the 0.85 danger threshold. And your efficient frontier position moves toward the optimal zone. This property improves your portfolio mathematically. That is how you use this output to make a decision."
Watch
Stop sharing your screen. Ask them to share theirs and open their copy of the Monte Carlo model. Give them a different property scenario to model themselves.
"Your turn. Share your screen and open your Monte Carlo model. I want you to model a different property — a small multifamily in Columbus, Ohio. 4 units. Value $620,000. Total monthly rent $4,400. Monthly expenses $1,600. Mortgage $420,000. Monthly payment $2,890. Interest rate 7.1% fixed. Drop those numbers in and run it. Tell me what the output shows."
Watch
Watch them enter the inputs and run the model. Do not correct small errors immediately — let them finish running it first, then address any issues.
Ask
After they run it — ask them to interpret the output themselves before you say anything.
"What does the output tell you? Does this Columbus property improve or worsen your portfolio mathematically? What happens to your Sharpe ratio and your efficient frontier position if you add it? Based purely on the numbers — would you buy it?"
Ask
Confirm independence before moving on.
"Next time a broker brings you a deal — before you say yes or no — can you run this model yourself and get a clear mathematical answer? What would make you more confident using it independently?"
Monte Carlo notes — input errors, interpretation issues, questions asked

Decision Framework
Minutes 45 – 58
Tool 3 · Decision Framework
0–5 Open
5–25 Dashboard
25–45 Monte Carlo
45–58 Framework
58–60 Close
Objective
Teach the client to apply their decision framework to real portfolio decisions independently. Apply two scenarios together — one buy or refinance scenario and one sell or rebalance scenario. They must navigate the framework themselves, not just follow your lead.
Do
Share your screen briefly. Open Tab 3 — the decision framework. Give a 1-minute overview of the structure — what sections it has and how to navigate it.
"This is your decision framework. It is 2–3 pages. It has four sections: Should I buy this property, Should I sell this property, Should I refinance, and Should I rebalance. Each section has a specific set of questions you answer with numbers — not feelings. Your answers tell you what to do. Let me show you how it works with a real scenario from your portfolio."
Do
Apply Scenario 1 — use the scenario you prepared in Phase 1. Walk through the relevant framework section yourself first, answering each question out loud using their actual data.
"Scenario 1 — your variable rate loan on Property 3 matures in Q3 2027. Should you refinance now or wait? Open the Refinancing section of the framework. Question 1: What is your current rate versus the available fixed rate? Question 2: What does your DSCR look like at a 150 basis point rate increase? Question 3: How many months until maturity? The framework says if the answers are [X] you act now. Let me show you how I work through it."
Watch
Ask them to share their screen. Present Scenario 2 — a different type of decision. Ask them to navigate the framework section themselves and reach a conclusion without your guidance.
"Your turn. Share your screen and open your decision framework document. Here is Scenario 2: A broker calls you tomorrow with an off-market Phoenix property similar to your existing Phoenix holdings — $520,000, similar tenant profile. Before you say yes or no you open the framework. Find the acquisition section. Work through every question using your real numbers and tell me what the framework says you should do."
Watch
Watch them navigate the framework. Do not prompt them unless they are completely stuck. The goal is to see them work through it independently — even if it takes longer than you would.
Ask
After they reach a conclusion — ask them to justify it with numbers not feelings.
"What did the framework tell you to do? And what specific number drove that conclusion? I want to hear the mathematical reason — not the intuitive reason."
Ask
Confirm independence with the most important question of the entire handoff session.
"From this point forward — every acquisition, every sale, every refinancing decision — will you open this framework before making a call? What would stop you from using it consistently? And what would make it easier to use every time?"
Framework notes — how they navigated it, conclusions reached, commitment level
